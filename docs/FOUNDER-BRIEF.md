# What the agent has to be

This is the founder's own statement of the goal, in their words. It is the *what*.
`FABLE-BRIEF.md` is the *how*: the instruments, the invariants and the traps.

Read this one first. If a change does not serve something below, it is not the work.

---

## The standard

Sage's platform is ready. The agent is the core, and the agent is what needs to be better.

The bar is a hundred million dollar agent. Not a demo, not a clever prototype. A founder with
millions of users and followers, putting up **$10,000 or $50,000**, testing a serious modern
product, should be able to rely on Sage's autonomy without supervising it. They need to know:

> **The right answers get paid. The wrong ones and the cheaters do not.**

That confidence is the product. Everything else is in service of it.

This has to be true on **web and Telegram equally**. Same agent, same quality, same result. A
founder must never get a worse outcome because of which door they came through.

And it has to be true for **any product**, not the handful Sage has been tested on. Any type,
any budget, including the newest and most complex web3 products.

---

## 1. Browsing and inspection

This is the foundation. If Sage does not really see the product, nothing downstream can be good.

- When Sage gets confused it restarts the whole thing. **Does it save what it already saw?**
  It should not throw away good work and start over.
- After it has looked at everything, the model needs to genuinely *know* what was seen when it
  designs the missions. Not just follow the goal text and ignore the exploration.
- It should work on every kind of product, not fail on whole categories.

## 2. Mission creation

- Missions must come from what Sage actually observed, not only from what the goal says.
- **Budget division has to be smart.** How many people should do this mission, given the budget
  and given whether the task is easy or hard? A difficult task deserves fewer testers paid more.
  An easy one can spread wider.
- A big budget should buy more and better testing, not absurd rewards.

## 3. The part Sage cannot do itself

This is important and general.

Sometimes the founder's goal requires something Sage is not able to do. Example: testing ClawUp
with a real budget, where the goal is that users must launch an agent. That needs buying credits
and paying for compute. Sage cannot spend money.

**It should still create missions around it.** With good budget division, because it is a
difficult task. And Sage should verify it through the evidence it requires from the tester before
paying.

Build it that way **for any product**. ClawUp is only the example.

## 4. Evidence and payouts

This is where trust is won or lost.

- Sage must be **smart about who to pay**. Not everyone who submits deserves payment. Legitimate,
  high-quality work must get paid, and so must anyone who genuinely did the task.
- A **beginner with weak or rough English** who really did the work and submitted real evidence
  must get paid. Poor writing is not fraud. Someone should not lose money for not being a good
  writer.
- People who did not do the work get nothing.
- If someone's answer is right but they explained it badly, **the agent should double check**
  rather than reject. A right answer should never fail because it was hard to put into words.
- Evidence checking has to adapt to what people actually submit, across many different products.

## 5. How to judge whether it is good enough

Test it and then think broadly about what is still missing, from three points of view:

- **A weak tester** — a beginner, poor English, first time, trying honestly.
- **An advanced tester** — experienced, thorough, and also the one most able to game it.
- **A founder** — spending real money and deciding whether to trust the result.

All of it tested. Not assumed.

## What this is for

So Sage can run its own campaigns, and so founders can be approached and offered a funded
campaign on their own product, of any type, tested by Sage end to end.

Everything through the agent. That is the point.
