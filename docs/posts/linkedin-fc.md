## Post 1 · Sunday · the opener (first post ever)
**Attachments:** carousel `10-sage-in-six-receipts.pdf` (or, as images: `01-landing.png`,
`02-explorer.png`, `05-receipt-first-autonomous-payout.png`, the OpenClaw certificate).

I have never posted here. Twenty-six days ago I joined the Future Caribbean buildathon, and today is the right day to start.

I build Sage. Sage is an AI agent that pays people for verified work — and turns every payment into a credit record a lender can read.

The problem it is built for is the one the finance track put on the table: 80 to 90% of Caribbean businesses are small, most lending is collateral-based, and moving money between islands costs 7 to 9% on more than $20bn a year. The real work happens, and nothing trustworthy records that it did.

What Sage does:
- A founder, a programme or a cooperative describes the work once, in its own currency — Jamaican dollars, TT dollars, EC dollars — and funds it once.
- The agent checks each deliverable itself and pays the person in digital dollars within minutes, inside spending limits enforced by code it cannot change.
- Every payment produces a public receipt, and every receipt lands on the worker's own cash-flow record — the file a lender can underwrite.
- It refuses. Work that does not meet the brief is declined with the reason on record. That refusal rate is what makes the record worth lending against.

The proof, all of it public:
- Live on two settlement rails since July, with real money: $63.60 settled across 39 verified payments to 24 people, 26 refusals on record, a median of under three minutes from submission to payment.
- First place at the OpenClaw bootcamp in August, judged on the same live system.
- 4,400 automated tests, and a public ledger where every number on the site is read from the transactions themselves.

Built solo. Thank you to Lily Dash and the Future Caribbean community for building a room where a system like this gets judged on whether the payouts hold up.

The system is built. What it needs now is the institutions it was built for. If you run an MSME programme, a cooperative, a payroll for contractors, or a lending desk that wants to price cash flow instead of collateral, I would value twenty minutes.

## Post 2 · Monday, submission day · the clearest explanation
**Attachments:** `07-work-record.png`, `08-lender-view.png`, `03-outcomes.png`, and the composer
screenshot from the demo (the J$10,000 grant compiled) — or the carousel if Post 1 used images.

Submission day for Sage at Future Caribbean. This is the clearest explanation I have of why it exists.

A market seller in Kingston gets paid for real work every week. None of it is on paper. No payslip, no filings, no bureau file — so when she needs $200 of working capital before the next order clears, the answer is collateral she does not have.

Sage was built so that the act of paying her creates the file.

- The buyer says the work once, in Jamaican dollars: "J$10,000 in two parts — half when her catalogue page is online, half when she posts her first customer review."
- Sage turns that into two milestones with a verification rule each, converts once at a stamped, source-attributed rate, and funds it.
- When she submits, the agent verifies the page itself and pays within minutes. The receipt is public. Her income is not.
- Each verified payment lands on her record: inflow over 30 and 90 days, distinct payers, tenure, verification pass rate — published formulas over receipts, never a score.
- A lender reads that record with one call and gets the numbers, not a PDF. The record carries a working-capital door: an advance sized from verified inflow, repaid automatically from the next payments.

Where it stands today: live with real money since July, 39 verified payments and 26 refusals on the public ledger, obligations in 14 currencies with the Caribbean first, sanctions screening on every payment path, and, since this morning, the first working-capital advance drawn against a seller's verified inflow and repaid in full by her next payout, the same day. Every receipt is public.

Every claim links to a page or a transaction a judge can open. Thank you to Future Caribbean for a track that asks for exactly that.

## Post 3 · Tuesday · the system that says no
**Attachments:** `02-explorer.png`, `06-receipt-private-leg-starknet.png` (as "a receipt"), `12-docs-compliance.png`.

The number I am proudest of on Sage's ledger is not the money paid. It is 26.

26 of the last 65 decisions our AI agent made about money were refusals — the page did not carry the worker's own marker, the text was copied, the brief was not met. Each one has the reason on the record next to the payments it did make.

Bankers understand this instinctively: a payment system that never declines is not a payment system. It is a leak. And a cash-flow record is only worth underwriting if the money behind it was verified before it moved.

How Sage decides:
- The agent reads the deliverable itself and proposes pay or refuse; it can never state an amount. The amount comes from the funded plan and is enforced by code the agent cannot change.
- Every person is screened against the sanctions list on every payment path.
- Public work asks each worker to prove they are one person, once, with no name or document — so a bounty for fifty people is earned by fifty people.
- Every decision leaves a receipt with the evidence it relied on.

That is the control environment a lender needs before the word "credit" means anything. Built for Future Caribbean's finance track, with the refusal ledger public from day one.

## Post 4 · Wednesday · the people doing the work
**Attachments:** `04-marketplace.png`, the identity clip or `09-wallet-graph.png`, `06-receipt-private-leg-starknet.png`.

Half of the people Sage has paid had never held a crypto wallet. They never needed one.

For Future Caribbean's finance track we made the worker's side as plain as a bank app, without the bank:
- Your organisation pastes its list and forwards each person a door on WhatsApp. You sign in with an email; Sage keeps a wallet for you.
- Do the work, submit the link. The agent verifies it and pays in minutes.
- Withdraw from inside Sage in one tap; you never buy gas or install anything.
- Your income stays yours: on the private rail, the receipt proves the payment happened without publishing who received it or what they hold.
- Every payment builds a record that is yours, that a lender can read, and that you can export.

24 people have been paid this way across 36 verified payments, from a Kingston-priced grant to product testing. The marketplace shows what is open right now, and the public wallet graph shows how the agent keeps a bounty for many people from being taken by one.

If you run a programme that pays freelancers, apprentices or micro-suppliers, this is the door they walk through.

## Post 5 · Thursday · fund it once
**Attachments:** `10-docs-operator.png`, the operator clip, `01-landing.png`.

The question every programme manager asked me at Future Caribbean: who runs this day to day?

Nobody. That is the point.

Sage's operator lets an organisation fund a treasury once and set ceilings — per campaign, per week, a floor it never spends below, a veto window. From there the agent decides what work to buy next, proposes each move with its reason and its price before a dollar leaves, and waits. You can stop any move. It can never spend past your ceilings, price its own work, or buy against a product you did not name.

It even proposes at zero: a new organisation sees the first move Sage would make before funding anything.

For a grant programme, a cooperative or an incubator, that is a fund that runs itself inside the rules a board approved — with every decision on a public ledger. Thank you Lily Dash and Future Caribbean for a buildathon that judged this on real payments rather than slides.
