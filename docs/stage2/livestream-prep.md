## "How does it actually work?"

> Three steps, and the agent does all of them.
>
> First it opens your product in a real browser, like a person would. It clicks around, signs up,
> tries things. It's actually using your product, not just reading the page.
>
> Then it writes the testing missions itself, based on what it saw. So if it found a signup flow, it
> writes a mission about the signup flow. It's not a template.
>
> Then people come and do those missions. They write up what they did. And the agent checks their
> account against what it saw itself when it was in there. If it lines up, they get paid. Usually in
> about two and a half minutes.

## "What's been the hardest part?"

Pick whichever comes to mind — both are true.

**The honest one:**

> Getting the agent to say no.
>
> It's easy to build something that pays people. It's hard to build something you'd trust to refuse
> someone. Because the moment it refuses a real person who did real work, they're angry, and they're
> right to be.
>
> So most of my time went into the agent's eyes, not its wallet. Making sure it actually saw the
> product properly, so when it judges someone's work it's comparing against something real.

**The one people remember:**

> I had a moment where I thought paid testers just don't give feedback. Four people got paid, I asked
> them for feedback afterwards, nobody replied. So I wrote it down as a finding — testers won't talk.
>
> It was completely wrong. They'd all written detailed feedback. Really good stuff, exact commands,
> bugs I didn't know about. It was sitting in their submissions the whole time. I just had never
> built a screen that showed it to me.
>
> So I'd concluded something about people when the actual problem was my own dashboard. That one
> stuck with me.

## If someone asks "isn't it risky to let an AI spend money?"

**This is the only place the technical answer belongs.** Don't bring it up yourself.

> Yeah, and that's why the agent doesn't hold the money.
>
> The money sits in a contract the founder owns. The agent can't take money out of it. All it can do
> is say "this person did this work, settle it" — and the contract works out the amount itself and
> checks its own limits.
>
> So the agent proposes and the contract decides. Even if someone got hold of my keys, they couldn't
> drain it. They could only ask, same as the agent.
