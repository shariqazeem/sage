# Product Growth Report

*Sage · OpenClaw Summer Builder Bootcamp · Stage 2*

## The original target

> *"Our first target is deliberately modest and real: one funded campaign with genuine payouts, then
> a steady rise in total USDC settled and in unique testers and founders, measured week over week."*

## Target status: MET

Seven campaigns were funded and paid out, not one. The north star metric we chose — total USDC
settled to verified testers — moved only through real, verified work, exactly as intended.

## Results

All figures are on GOAT Mainnet. Every payout is a public transaction with a receipt page citing the
evidence it was paid for, so the entire scoreboard can be checked without our cooperation.

## The two campaigns

**clawup.org, 14 August.** Six testers filed eight reports, four were paid $5.26 each. Every one of
them signed up to ClawUp, created a working agent, and connected a live messaging channel.

**sagepays.xyz, 16 August.** Fourteen testers submitted, ten were paid $2.50 each. The campaign
filled and paid out completely **in forty-five minutes**, with no human approving any of the ten
payments.

## What we learned

**Supply is easy. Demand is the hard side.** We planned to seed testers first and it worked far
better than expected — ten strangers did careful work for $2.50 within the hour. The constraint on
the north star is budget, not tester availability.

**The refusals are the product.** Forty-eight percent of submissions did not result in a payout,
each with a written reason. An agent that can only say yes is not judging anything; the refusals are
what make the payouts mean something.

**A metric reading zero is a claim about your instrument first.** We recorded "paid testers give no
feedback" as a finding. It was wrong — their feedback was inside their submissions the whole time,
and we had simply never built the screen that showed it. Fixing the screen produced thirteen pieces
of real feedback in a few hours.

## Founder activation

Achieved as the proposal defined it — *"a founder's first funded campaign"* — with six distinct
wallets creating campaigns. The honest qualifier: the funded ones are ours. Two outside founder
conversations are live and pricing agreed, with screenshots attached; neither has funded yet, so
neither is counted in any figure above.
