## 1. Direction check against the track brief

The brief, condensed: *"Strong teams build financial infrastructure. Weak teams build apps. Does
your system change outcomes — lower costs, faster settlement, expanded access to capital, more
regional capital flow?"* Its CORE is the **MSME Credit Layer**: aggregate fragmented data →
real-time credit profiles → lending without collateral → working capital & trade finance →
integrate with lenders. Then payments (<1% fees), regional FX, RegTech, diaspora capital.

Where Sage already IS that system — say it exactly this way, everywhere:

| brief asks | Sage today | how we say it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cash-flow-based profiles from fragmented data | the verified work record: every payout verified before it moved, published formulas, no score | "In markets where the books don't exist, Sage writes the book as the work happens." |
| lending without collateral | Sage Advance: capacity = multiple × verified inflow, disbursed as a claim link, repaid by a waterfall on the next verified payout | "Capital moves before the next invoice clears — under the same policy that releases every other dollar." |
| working capital & trade finance | milestone grants = supplier credit shaped by delivery; the advance = working capital | "A supplier gets paid when the delivery verifies. That is trade finance with the paperwork generated by the payment itself." |
| integrate with lenders | `/lender` dashboard, `/api/record/<wallet>` JSON, CSV, signed floor attestations | "Decision support first, API second — both live." |
| payments <1%, real-time | 0% taken from recipients, median 3 min submission→settled, two mainnet rails | the /outcomes readings, computed live |
| works with fragmented real-world data | the record is BUILT from settlement rows across two rails and 14 denominations | "Aggregation is what the ledger does; nothing is self-reported." |
| regional FX | obligations priced in J$/TT$/EC$/Bds$…, converted once at a stamped rate; netting becomes real when two-currency obligations exist | ONE honest slide — never a claim |
| RegTech | OFAC screening on every payout, refusals on the record, receipts | one line |
| diaspora capital | a lender supplies the multiple; the pot funds advances | "the first lender is us; the pipe is open" |

Direction is right. Two things the brief says that we should close THIS week (section 2, builds
F1–F2) and one we say honestly as roadmap (regional FX netting).

**STRK20 direction:** the jury wants privacy added to products WITH users. We have 29 mainnet

## 5. The campaign — one $25 gig, on Starknet, private

**Why one, and why this shape.** One campaign is one headline and one link. Five people paid
privately is five rows on the explorer and five records — the STRK20 jury sees usage, the FC jury
sees the loop. So: **one $25 gig · 8–10 slots ($2.50–$3.13 each) · Starknet · listed publicly** — and it pays us back: each verified page is a testimonial + backlink naming Sage at sagepays.xyz.

- **Title:** Try a private payout and write up what happened
- **What must be true:** the worker publishes a public page (blog, Notion, GitHub gist, dev.to…)
  that (a) explains in their own words how Sage paid them without their income appearing against
  their address, (b) contains the exact phrase **"paid privately through Sage"**, (c) carries
  their submitting wallet address (the artifact marker).
- **How Sage verifies:** the link they publish (artifact_url, wallet marker) — deterministic
  pre-gate, then the judge reads the account.
- **Who:** anyone · 8–10 people · $2.50–$3.13 each · priced in USD · Public marketplace (NOT invite-only).
- **Where it goes:** composer → Pay for work → Gig → 5 people → verify "the link they publish" →
  Private-capable rail → fund $25 → live. The full inputs are in `docs/posts/CAMPAIGN-25.md`.
- **If Starknet or FC repost:** a second $25 as a **milestone grant priced in J$** — two tranches
  to one Caribbean freelancer or seller ("publish your catalogue page" / "post your first customer
  review") — the FC showpiece, which also feeds the grant film.
